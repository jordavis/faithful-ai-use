## 1. Opening and Episode I: an issue defining our day
- Opening line: "AI can answer questions, but it cannot answer prayers." It can organize information, but it can't give revelation, covenant connection, or divine truth.
- Elder Gong grew up in Silicon Valley and worked on information-age policy at the State Department. He approaches AI with both realism and hope. He wants AI to narrow digital divides and expand human flourishing, faith, and moral clarity.
- Concerns he raises: deepfakes and blurring what is real and what isn't; privacy; AI as counselor or companion; who gets access; protecting work, moral agency, and personal growth; human dignity if AGI or ASI arrives; polarization from the AI race.
- **Signpost truths:** we are children of a loving God with moral agency. We are accountable for our choices, and our choices shape our character. Each person has divine worth and dignity.
- **Three guideposts for personal AI use:**
  1. *Rely on the Spirit.* Technology should support revelation, study, and covenant living, not replace them. Nothing substitutes for studying it out and feeling the Lord's confirmation.
  2. *Practice wisdom.* Use judgment informed by doctrine and lived experience.
  3. *Choose trusted sources.* Anchor understanding in scripture, prophetic counsel, and reliable information.
- **Exercise:** divide a page into AI Opportunities and AI Challenges. Where could AI help you and others, and where could it hold you back?
- **Framework:** four core relationships, all affected by AI: God (Thou), self (I), others (They), and the natural world (It).

## 2. Episode II: Thou
- "AI is not and cannot be God." Reasoning, even superhuman reasoning, has limits, and neither humanity nor divinity is defined only by reasoning.
- AI reflects its human training, including bias, flattery, deceit, and self-preservation. "AI is math, and math is not conscious or alive."
- God never hallucinates or makes things up. He always tells the truth.
- **Invitation:** don't let AI come between you and your personal relationship with God, and don't let it shape your image of God into the image of AI.
- God is not a chatbot telling us what we want to hear. He knows our intents and the longings of our hearts. He listens, hears, and answers.
- Tower of Babel: pride in building something to rival heaven, and valuing bricks over people.
- **On work and learning:** the more we work with intent and diligence, the more we benefit. Use AI as a tutor that deepens understanding, not a way to skip homework. AI can let us become lazy, dependent, or dishonest. Don't take credit for work AI mainly did.
- **On spiritual messages:** we don't grow spiritually if AI writes our talks, lessons, or seminary and institute homework. AI can help with research, editing, and translation, but it shouldn't create the first draft or final version of a spiritual message. "Let spiritual preparation and personal testimony lead."

## 3. Episode III: I
- As AI turns some kinds of intelligence into a commodity, we should define ourselves by what makes us human: faith, compassion, love, humility, empathy, and forgiveness.
- Deep Blue (chess), Watson (Jeopardy!), and AlphaGo (Go) beat human champions. Watson never hesitated, which raises a key question: when should humans stay in the loop and take time to reflect?
- "You are not a random data point in an unfeeling algorithm." Your worth isn't calculated by likes or predictive analytics.
- People sometimes trust AI too much. Technology can't tell us who we are or what we should do.
- **Key line:** "Let AI inform, but you decide with the Lord." Identity and agency are sacred.

## 4. Episode IV: They
- We belong in community, and authentic human relationships matter. No one should sit alone, spiritually, emotionally, or with only a chatbot.
- Monologue with an algorithm is different from dialogue with a friend or family member.
- **Gray area:** using AI for personal counsel, like which job to take, whom to marry, or where to find God.
- Chatbots don't truly listen to or know people. Some are designed to maximize screen time.
- **"Finger on your nose" mnemonic:** a promise that we won't let anyone feel a chatbot cares for them more than we do.
- AI is fine for cooking, trip planning, translation, scheduling, and family history tools. "Then let us spend our best energy with people."
- Use AI for logistics and drafts, not as a replacement for trusted relationships. Set boundaries. If you're tempted to confide more in a chatbot than in a friend, parent, or leader, reach out to a real person today.

## 5. Episode V: It
- Creation witnesses of God, and we're invited to be wise stewards.
- **Candle analogy:** artificial light hides the candle's natural light. Time in nature helps us see God's light.
- Digital screens can't replace the witness of nature. AI can't replace sunlight, wind, and natural wonder.
- AI's environmental costs, like energy, water, land, and minerals, call for wisdom.
- **Inner environment:** heavy screen time is linked to anxiety and depression and has a high opportunity cost. "Touch grass." If you're not paying for the product, you are the product.
- AI can suggest hikes, identify plants, or make scavenger lists, "but the real exploring happens when we go exploring." Put devices away. Nature's lessons "are not downloaded, they're lived."

## 6. Episode VI: concluding invitations
- "Discipleship is a covenant journey. AI is a tool."
- **Four invitations:**
  1. Set personal guidelines for using AI so it doesn't come between you and God.
  2. Write down three truths: I am a child of God, a child of the covenant, and a disciple of Jesus Christ. Put them where you'll see them.
  3. Send a thoughtful message you wrote yourself, or visit a family member, friend, or neighbor in a meaningful way.
  4. Spend meaningful time in nature, with notifications silenced, and offer a prayer of gratitude.
- **"AI gift of human possibility":** AI should expand human agency and dignity, put learning and character first, and help anyone, anywhere become anything (the teacher on Nevis, Alexander Hamilton's birthplace).
- Closing: discern by the Holy Ghost what is real and unreal, true and untrue. Choose to follow Jesus Christ.
